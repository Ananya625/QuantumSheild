    Bob's Receiver Station

    Alice sends the polarized photon through a fiber optic cable. Bob receives it but does not know which basis Alice used. Bob must choose a basis (+ or ×) at random to measure it.

    - If Bob chooses the same basis as Alice, he measures the correct bit with 100% certainty.
    - If he chooses a different basis, he collapses the photon state and gets a random bit (0 or 1 with 50% probability).

    Eve Intercepts the Channel

    What happens if an eavesdropper, Eve, tries to intercept the photons? According to quantum physics (the No-Cloning Theorem), Eve cannot copy a photon. She must measure it.

    Eve measures each photon in a random basis (+ or ×). If she measures, she collapses the qubit's wave. She then sends the collapsed photon onward to Bob. This introduces disturbance and errors in Bob's measurements!

    QBER (Quantum Bit Error Rate) Analysis

    To detect Eve, Alice and Bob publicly share and compare a random sample of their sifted key bits (e.g., 6 bits). They calculate the QBER (Quantum Bit Error Rate):

    QBER = (Incorrect Bits) / (Compared Bits)

    - No Eve: QBER should be 0% (in an ideal noiseless channel).
    - Eve Present: QBER rises to a theoretical average of 25%, because Eve guesses the wrong basis 50% of the time, causing Bob's measurements to fail 50% of the time.
